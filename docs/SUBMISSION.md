### What is Caprae's mission?
Caprae Capital's mission is to "make others great through entrepreneurship". It partners with founders and operators and pairs capital with deep operational expertise, so good businesses become great, lasting ones. Unlike firms that win mainly through financial engineering, Caprae treats an acquisition as the start of a roughly seven-year journey, where most of the value is created after the deal.

That shows in its model: M&A-as-a-Service and SaaS tools that support companies post-acquisition, with practical AI as a central lever for better decisions and leaner operations. The purpose isn't only returns. It's businesses that are stronger for their employees, customers and communities, and entrepreneurs who get the support to succeed.

### Why do you want to work at Caprae Capital?
*(Personalise.)* Suggested angle: you want to build software that is used the same week it ships, as SaaSquatch was: 1,200 sign-ups in its first three weeks. Caprae is founder- and operator-first, it builds its own tools instead of renting them, and it values horsepower over mileage. That matches how you like to work: own a problem end to end, talk to users, ship fast. Mention one specific moment from this challenge, such as discovering that the demo's email generator needed 60 hand-typed words of context and deciding to eliminate that step.

### How is Caprae changing the ETA space and broader PE?
Traditional private equity often relies on leverage and multiple expansion. Traditional search funds leave a single first-time CEO largely on their own after closing. Caprae sits between the two: an operator-led, family-office structure that stays hands-on after acquisition and brings shared operating playbooks, a network of advisors, and technology. That includes AI deployed across the portfolio.

It is also lowering the cost of entry for acquisition entrepreneurs. SaaSquatch gives searchers, students and operators affordable sourcing that used to require expensive data stacks. Its public writing on governance, searcher-CEO termination issues and culture (#BleedandBuild) pushes the industry toward more transparency and better alignment between investors and operators.
